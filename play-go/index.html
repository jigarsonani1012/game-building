How to Play Go

The ancient strategy game — simple rules, endless depth
Step 01 • Placement
Intersections, Not Squares
Black moves first. Players take turns placing stones directly on the line intersections (grid crossings), never inside the squares. Once placed, stones never move.
Step 02 • Capturing
Liberties & Atari
Empty adjacent points are liberties (breathing room). Fill all of a stone's liberties and it is captured and removed! When a stone has only 1 liberty left, it is in atari.
Step 03 • Survival
Two Eyes (Invincibility)
Playing where you have zero liberties is suicide (illegal) unless it captures. Because of this, any group with two separate internal eyes can never be captured!
Step 04 • Scoring
Territory & Komi
When neither player wants to play, both pass. Players score points for each empty intersection they fully surround (territory). White receives 6.5 komi to balance Black's first-move advantage.

⚔️
Atari Go (First to Capture)
The fast, exciting capture battle for all skill levels
🎯 First to N Captures Wins
🏁 Instant Victory Condition
Unlike Classic Go where you surround board territory, in Atari Go the goal is pure combat: the first player to capture the target amount of stones (e.g. 1 stone or 3 stones) instantly wins the game!
💡 Why Play Atari Go?
It's the ultimate training ground for liberties, cross-cuts, ladders, and atari defense. You don't have to calculate territory or end-game passes — pure tactical intuition.

👑
"Corners are Gold, Sides are Silver, Center is Grass"

In the corner, you only need 4 stones to secure 4 points of territory. On the side you need 6 stones, and in the open center you need 8 stones. Always claim corners first!

🛡️
Don't Follow the Opponent Blindly (Tenuki)

You don't always have to reply to where your opponent just played. If their move is small and does not threaten immediate death, play a big point elsewhere (tenuki).

👁️
Shape Before Capture

Chasing an opponent's stones blindly often leaves your own groups thin and vulnerable. Build solid, connected stone chains and give your groups room to create two eyes.
