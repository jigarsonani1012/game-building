How to play Go

1
Take turns placing stones

Black plays first. Stones go on the intersections, not the squares, and never move once placed.

2
Liberties & capture

The empty points next to a stone (or a connected group) are its liberties. Fill all of them and the group is captured and removed. A group with one liberty left is in atari.

3
No suicide, no repetition

You can't play a stone that would have zero liberties (unless it captures). The ko rule stops you from immediately recapturing a single stone that just captured a single stone — play elsewhere first.

4
Ending & counting

When neither player sees a useful move, both pass. Dead stones are removed, then each player counts their territory (area rules also count stones on the board). White gets komi to compensate for moving second.

Tips for beginners
  • Corners first, then sides, then the center — it's easier to make territory there.
  • Don't chase every stone. Capturing is a tool; surrounding space is the goal.
  • A group with two separate eyes can never be captured.
  • Use Estimate to see who's ahead and Hints when you're stuck.
  • On touch screens, press and slide your finger to aim, then lift to place.

The capture game: first to capture the target number of stones wins. Perfect for learning liberties and atari without worrying about territory.
